Can smaller pixels make SAR sharper?

Imagine a single bright point in a synthetic radar image. Predict what happens to its blur when you change pixel spacing alone. Then try changing bandwidth while holding pixel spacing fixed.

At 100 MHz, the first null is 1.50 m from the peak. The example samples the response every 0.50 m.

See the equation and its assumptions

d = c/(2B)  ·  A(x) = |sinc(x/d)|

Here x is slant-range offset in metres, B is bandwidth in hertz, and c is the speed of light. The normalized sinc is sin(πu)/(πu) and equals 1 at zero. The first null is at x = ±d. This model uses one point, a rectangular effective spectrum, and perfect focus. Look angle, weighting, motion, and azimuth aperture affect the resolution of a real image.

At 100 MHz, switching from 1.00 m to 0.25 m spacing puts more dots on the same curve. Doubling bandwidth to 200 MHz moves the first null from 1.50 m to 0.75 m. Read the SAR engineering reference.

Learning roadmap

The engineering reference covers selected foundations. Each series below has a concrete next lesson that connects an observable effect to an equation and a check.

  • Signal and waveform physics

    Read now. Pulse compression, sampling, and range–Doppler maps.

    Next lessons. Rectangular pulses, up/down linear chirps, nonlinear chirps, stepped frequency, and phase coding. Compare their matched responses and show where each sampled or stepped workflow applies.

    Start with echoes

  • Detection and tracking

    Read now. Local thresholding, plots, and the scan Kalman model.

    Next lessons. Uncertainty ellipses, gating, and two crossing objects. Compare nearest-neighbor and one-to-one matching, then introduce maneuver and probabilistic association references.

    Start with plots and tracks

  • Resource management

    Next lessons. Allocate a shared clock across a synthetic survey. Use a timeline to show task requests, finite capacity, priority, deadlines, and the effects of deferring work.

  • RF interference and receiver resilience

    Next lessons. Learn the vocabulary behind electronic counter-countermeasures (ECCM). Use an offline coexistence example to separate noise, clutter, and interference and compare how a receiver measurement changes under a controlled disturbance.

  • Scene generation and RF scattering

    Read now. Shared geometry, terrain, and time.

    Next lessons. Build a point, a surface patch, and a synthetic terrain scene. Compare statistical clutter with scene-generated echoes using the same view and explain which material and motion assumptions change the result.

    Start with the shared world

  • Atmosphere and EO/IR image formation

    Read now. Infrared radiance, emissivity, and the display palette.

    Next lessons. Compare the same surface through clear air and haze. Distinguish RF path loss and atmospheric attenuation from optical transmission and path radiance. Then show how optics and exposure shape the image.

    Start with thermal imaging

  • Imaging, fusion, and evidence

    Try now. Change bandwidth and pixel spacing in the SAR lesson.

    Next lessons. Compare flat and terrain-focused images, then follow one observation through fusion, replay, and scoring with timestamps and uncertainties visible.
