King Farm in 3D

The S³ Workbench's 3D studio, running in your browser. An airborne radar circles King Farm in Rockville, Maryland, over USGS aerial imagery and OpenStreetMap buildings. It watches cars, trucks, a drone and an airliner among the neighbourhood's buildings. Play the scenario, orbit the scene, switch between visual, infrared, radar and tactical lenses, and open the radar display.

This is the same Cesium studio the Workbench serves, with self-hosted CesiumJS, USGS and USDA NAIP imagery, OpenStreetMap buildings and S³ synthetic models. The terrain under King Farm is a synthetic stand-in, and the lenses are display representations, not calibrated sensor imagery.

AESA radar

Watch a four-face AESA search and track.

A ground radar with four electronically steered faces searches, confirms and tracks in one 60-second run. Its fast statistical detection model drives the whole run, and the page shows where to trust it: doubtful dwells are re-run as signals, the mast camera renders thermal imagery, and ground tracks cue an airborne SAR.

Inside S³

From scene to track

Workflows retain different products for inspection. The recorded radar example shows the range–Doppler result, observations and tracker states.

  1. SceneFictional motion in a synthetic scene or a scene with public geographic context.
  2. TargetsSelected target models include 3D geometry and modeled scattering points.
  3. SensorsSelected scenarios simulate airborne radar, ground radar or infrared sensing.
  4. Signal chainPulse compression, range–Doppler processing, clutter suppression and CFAR detection.
  5. TracksDetections become tracks; truth, observations and estimates stay side by side for checking.

Real places, public data

Some scenes use public terrain and maps as context. Terrain masking and clutter depend on the selected model.

Radar and infrared together

Selected scenarios explore synchronized radar and infrared views of the same scene.

Requirements to evidence

Features are tied to written requirements and automated tests, in the spirit of model-based systems engineering.
