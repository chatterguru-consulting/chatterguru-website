Founder & Lab

I’m Chatterpaul Joseph.

I started in sonar and spent the next two decades building and testing sensor systems. At Chatterguru, I use Sensor Scenario Studio to explore those engineering questions through fictional scenarios anyone can inspect.

Chatterpaul Joseph

Founder and principal systems engineer

My work has covered sonar, radar seekers, airborne electronic warfare, satellite communications, infrared missile warning and phased-array radar, on programs for the Navy, Army, Air Force and Space Force, and now the Department of Homeland Security.

I was born in Guyana and grew up in Pawtucket, Rhode Island. I founded Chatterguru in 2022, and the practice is based in Rockville, Maryland, near Washington, DC. Along the way I have led small engineering teams and mentored junior engineers and high-school students.

Career

Twenty years of sensors, from sonar to space.

  1. Today

    Radar for homeland security

    CACI

    I serve as a radar subject-matter expert for CACI on its work for U.S. Customs and Border Protection, part of the Department of Homeland Security.

  2. 2026

    Phased-array radar

    Leonardo DRS · Senior Principal Systems Engineer

    I was a radar-algorithm specialist for tactical phased-array radars: algorithm development, detection and tracking performance analysis, and investigation of field and test anomalies. I also coached and mentored engineers on the team.

  3. 2024 to today

    Chatterguru's lab

    I run the lab where Sensor Scenario Studio is built.

  4. 2022 to 2024

    Satellite communications and missile warning

    General Dynamics Mission Systems · Chatterguru's first engagement

    Chatterguru began here. I founded the company to work corp-to-corp as an independent contractor, and spent two years as a senior principal systems engineer supporting General Dynamics Mission Systems on two ground systems.

    MUOS, the military's narrowband satellite communications system: terminal and broadcast-service design, link-budget trade studies and an end-to-end communications model.

    Tranche 1 Tracking Layer, the Space Development Agency's infrared missile-warning satellites in low Earth orbit: tracking specialist for mission data processing, and a prototype global sensor-coverage map for operators.

  5. 2016 to 2022

    Airborne electronic warfare

    Northrop Grumman · Lead System Architect

    I led the architecture of RF electronic warfare systems for aircraft, guiding engineers across software, firmware and test. I used model-based systems engineering to connect requirements, interfaces and verification, and led an end-to-end digital twin that let the team assess a design before hardware was built. I also worked on RF links and payload concepts for satellites.

  6. 2008 to 2016

    Radar seekers and interceptors

    Raytheon Missile Systems · System Algorithm Development and Production Lead

    I developed and tested radar-seeker algorithms for detection, tracking and target recognition, and the flight simulations that predict a seeker's performance before a test and are checked against it afterwards. I worked on Small Diameter Bomb II and was the radar modeler for AI3, an interceptor built to defend against rockets, artillery and mortars, where I also developed the jam-resistant datalink from the ground radar to the interceptors. I supported more than 24 flight-test missions.

    In production, I took over the factory calibration routines and used design of experiments to raise yield by more than 30 percent, worth over $2 million a year.

  7. Early years

    Sonar and signal processing

    Graduate work at the University of Rhode Island on statistical signal processing for sonar, where I also taught MATLAB, led to my first engineering job, on Navy submarine and torpedo sonar systems.

The lab

What the lab is building.

When a simulator draws a track, I want to see the observations behind it. That’s one reason I’m building Sensor Scenario Studio (S³). The recorded radar demo lets you step through a fictional run and compare where two simulated targets were, what the radar reported, and where the tracker put them.

The 3D scenes let you explore King Farm and South Mountain with public map imagery, fictional traffic and simulated sensor views.
