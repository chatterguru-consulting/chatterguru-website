Shared world and terrain

The scene supplies geometry and time to the sensor models. A radar pulse and a thermal exposure can occur at different times, so a synchronized view has to preserve the timestamp and pose of each observation.

pworld(t) = R(t) pbody + porigin(t)

Use the same coordinate frame and time in both the rotation R and translation p. For example, an offset of 2 m along local east maps to a world point 2 m east of the origin when the local axes align.

Check in replay. Confirm the coordinate frame, terrain vertical reference, time of the last observation, and treatment of missing elevations. A bare-earth elevation grid and a lidar point cloud represent different geometry. Inspect the shared scene interfaces · USGS 3DEP product definitions

Waveforms and clutter

A matched filter compares received complex samples with a delayed copy of the transmitted waveform. A statistical clutter model can draw background values for an experiment. A scene-based clutter model builds returns from geometry and surface assumptions before processing.

y[ℓ] = ∑n x[n] s*[n − ℓ]

x is the recorded signal, s is the reference waveform, * denotes complex conjugation, and ℓ is lag in samples. In a noiseless single-echo example with a matching reference, the correlation peaks at the echo lag.

Check in replay. Identify where clutter enters the chain and whether it retains phase. Compare repeated runs with the same geometry and seed when isolating a single change. Retain the measured response and processing settings. Follow the sensor data flow

Range–Doppler processing

Pulse compression produces complex samples by range. A Fourier transform across pulses maps those samples into Doppler frequency. The window changes the width of peaks and how much energy leaks into nearby bins.

ΔRbin = c/(2fs)  ·  ΔfD = PRF/N

In this evenly sampled example, fs is the range sample rate in Hz, PRF is in Hz, N is the pulse count, and c is the speed of light. With 16 pulses at a PRF of 1,600 Hz, adjacent Doppler bins are 100 Hz apart. Bandwidth and weighting determine what the image can resolve.

Check in replay. Look for metres on the range axis, hertz on the Doppler axis, and the declared magnitude or power scale. Compare display settings and sampling before comparing images. Read the implemented RDM equations

Detection and tracking

The documented cell-averaging detector estimates local background from training cells outside the guard region. The processor can consolidate threshold crossings into plots. A tracker then associates observations across time and updates its state.

T = α · mean(training magnitudes)  ·  y = z − Hxpred

In the detector example, a mean magnitude of 2 and a multiplier α of 3 produce a threshold of 6 in the same magnitude units. The multiplier is a direct setting. A false-alarm probability requires its own statistical assumptions or measurements. In the Kalman update, the innovation y is the observation z minus the predicted measurement Hxpred.

Check in replay. Inspect detections and tracks separately. A coasting track carries a prediction from past observations. The recorded scene truth is available for scoring. Read the detector and edge policy · Read the tracker equations

SAR image formation

Synthetic aperture radar (SAR) coherently combines returns collected at different positions. Backprojection evaluates the delay and phase for each image coordinate. Using terrain heights changes that geometry, especially where the terrain differs from the flat focus plane.

τ = 2R/c  ·  δRslant ≈ c/(2B)

R is slant range in metres, τ is round-trip time in seconds, and B is bandwidth in Hz. An idealized 100 MHz waveform gives about 1.50 m nominal slant-range resolution. A 0.25 m pixel grid would sample that response more finely. Measure resolution from a point-target response along the stated axis and with the actual weighting.

Check in replay. Compare flat and terrain-focused views from the same history where available. Check terrain NoData, shadow, layover, image axes, and point-response width. In this synthetic reference, surface scattering uses simplified assumptions. Read the sampled-SAR equations and scope · View available scenes

Thermal imaging

The radiometric model integrates Planck radiance over a declared medium- or long-wave infrared band. The sensor receives emitted and reflected radiance after atmospheric transmission, plus path radiance. A display palette maps stored values to screen colors.

Lsensor = τatm[ε Lbb(T) + (1 − ε)Lbg] + Lpath

ε and τatm are dimensionless emissivity and transmission. Radiance terms use the same band and units (W·m−2·sr−1). T is in kelvin. With ε = 1, the reflected-background contribution vanishes in this opaque diffuse model.

Check in replay. Read the band, exposure time, and validity mask before inspecting colors. Visible and short-wave Studio display modes have their own rendering path. Read the radiometric equations and validity rules

LiDAR and observation fusion

These are theory references. They describe what a measured example should demonstrate when a public replay and its evidence are available.

LiDAR time of flight

R = cΔt/2

A 150 ns round trip corresponds to about 22.5 m in air. An example should report which return was measured, the coordinate frame, and how missing or multiple returns are represented. A point cloud used to build scenery has different provenance from a point cloud a simulated sensor emits.

Uncertainty in fusion

σ−2fused = σ−21 + σ−22

This scalar example assumes two independent, unbiased estimates of the same quantity. Two independent position measurements with 2 m standard deviation give a fused standard deviation of about 1.41 m. Shared inputs can correlate the errors. A public evidence example should identify the input sources, time alignment, uncertainty assumptions, and a correlated-input control.

For an experiment, keep the scenario and source revision, the observation clock, the processing settings, the output identity, and the evidence used to interpret it. Two display views can share a saved recording and still use different palettes, geometry, or layer selections.

RMSE = √[∑i ‖pestimated,i − preference,i‖² / N]

This basic review measure uses N matched positions at comparable times and in the same frame. Residual distances of 1 m and 3 m give an RMSE of about 2.24 m. Report missed and unmatched observations separately so the matched-pair error can be interpreted in context.
